Check the source behind each teaching claim

An evidence-checking workflow for editors who review medical question explanations.

MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD

Pick the sentence that could change the answer

Underline the claim that justifies the keyed option. Then write down its population, setting, comparison and outcome. A source can cover the topic while leaving one of those details unsupported.

Compare the wording with the source

Consider a fictional explanation that says a strategy benefits all adults. Its source studied a specific subgroup in one setting. Record the gap and narrow the claim to the studied population if that still answers the question. If narrowing the claim changes the rationale for the key, send the item back for review.

Keep a retrievable citation

  1. Record the reference title and date alongside the DOI, URL or stable identifier.
  2. Identify the section, table or recommendation that supports the sentence.
  3. Check for corrections or an updated guideline if the answer depends on current practice.
  4. Have another reviewer confirm that they can find the support and independently reproduce the reasoning.

Use the MedQ-AI question-review record to log the revision and the resource matching rubric when selecting follow-up teaching material.
