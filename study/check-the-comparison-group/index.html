Ask what the comparison group actually did

When an education study reports better performance with a new tool, look at the activities in both groups. The comparison determines which question the study can answer.

Describe both learning experiences

Consider two fictional study plans for a tool that selects follow-up questions after an incorrect answer. In each study, learners take the same type of assessment at the same planned follow-up time.

Two possible comparisons for adaptive question practice
DesignNew-tool groupComparison group
A45 minutes of questions, explanations, and instructor discussion15 minutes reading an article independently
B30 minutes of questions selected from previous responses, with explanations30 minutes of questions in a fixed order, with the same explanation format

Match the interpretation to the design

Design A compares learning experiences that differ in time, activity, feedback, and instructor contact. If the groups later score differently, the study team needs to consider those features together. Design B makes the activities more similar while changing how questions are selected. Readers would still need to check the learners’ starting knowledge, question coverage, assignment process, and how outcomes were measured.

Make a short comparison record

  • What material did each group receive?
  • How long did they use it, and was use measured?
  • What feedback or instructor access did each group have?
  • Could either group access the other group’s material?
  • Were assessment timing and scoring handled the same way?

Use the answers to write one sentence describing the comparison. For Design A, that could be “A longer question-and-discussion session was compared with a shorter independent reading session.” The sentence makes the tested difference explicit before you consider the outcome.

Cochrane’s handbook explains why the intervention and comparator need clear definitions, including delivery, duration, and accompanying activities. See Chapter 3 on defining comparisons. Pair this record with the follow-up participation guide when reading an education study.
