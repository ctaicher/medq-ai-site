Check who came back for the follow-up quiz

When an education study reports scores on a delayed quiz, find out how many learners took the quiz in each group. A result calculated among people who returned can be hard to interpret if follow-up differed by group. The CONSORT 2025 reporting guideline asks authors of randomized trials to identify how many people were assigned to each group, how many had data for an outcome, and how the analysis handled missing data.

Work through a fictional result

Suppose 50 learners were randomly assigned to each of two study schedules. Four weeks later, an identical quiz was offered to both groups. The table shows the numbers for that hypothetical quiz.

Learners assigned5050
Learners who took the quiz4025
Learners who passed3020
Pass rate among quiz takers75%80%

Schedule B has a higher pass rate among people who took the quiz. It also has 25 learners with missing results, compared with 10 in Schedule A. You would want to know why learners missed the quiz and how the investigators handled those missing outcomes before drawing a conclusion about the schedules.

Check how much the missing scores could matter

If every missing learner in Schedule A would have failed, 30 of the original 50 would pass. If every missing learner would have passed, the count would be 40 of 50. Those extreme assumptions give 60% to 80%. Apply the same exercise to Schedule B and the range is 40% to 90%. These are illustrative bounds under extreme assumptions, with no estimate of statistical uncertainty.

Find three things in the paper

  1. Find the prespecified follow-up date and outcome. A score immediately after a lesson and a score several weeks later answer different questions.
  2. Find the count with measured outcomes in each group and the reasons for missing scores, if given.
  3. Read how the analysis handled missing data and whether the conclusion changed under other assumptions.

See the CONSORT 2025 statement and its explanation on reporting participant flow and missing outcomes. For an individual study plan, use the MedQ-AI two-week retention log to record when you planned to check recall and when you actually did.
