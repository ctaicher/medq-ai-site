Check a learning-study headline against the planned outcome

A study can report several useful results. To interpret its main claim, find the outcome and assessment time that the investigators planned to treat as primary. Then compare that plan with the result emphasized in the report.

Put the planning record beside the results

In a fictional randomized study, the plan finalized before unblinded outcome analysis identifies a six-week knowledge-retention assessment as primary and an immediate post-session assessment as secondary. Both use a 100-point scale. The report includes the following hypothetical between-group differences, calculated as Method A minus Method B.

Fictional plan and results for two assessment times
AssessmentPlanned roleReported difference and 95% CI
Six-week knowledge assessmentPrimary+2 points, CI −3 to +7
Immediate knowledge assessmentSecondary+12 points, CI +7 to +17

A headline about a 12-point advantage refers to the immediate secondary assessment. It does not describe the six-week result. The six-week interval includes both lower and higher scores with Method A, so this result leaves uncertainty about the difference at that time.

Keep the result’s label attached

A useful summary could say “The immediate assessment favored Method A by 12 points. On the planned primary six-week assessment, the estimated difference was two points, with a confidence interval from three points lower to seven points higher.” The secondary finding can be reported while preserving which outcome was primary.

Check the instrument and analysis as well as the outcome name. An end-of-course score, a change from baseline, and a pass-rate analysis are different result definitions. Find which one was planned and whether the report explains any change.

Read changes in context

Cochrane’s guidance on selection of the reported result recommends comparing reports with prespecified analysis intentions. When a protocol, registration, or statistical analysis plan is available, record its version and timing relative to access to unblinded outcome data.

Plans can change for legitimate reasons. Look for what changed, when, and why, and whether both planned and additional analyses are clearly identified. A discrepancy is a reason to seek clarification. It does not establish the authors’ motives or prove that the result is wrong.

Make a short comparison record

  • The primary outcome’s measurement tool, time point, and analysis.
  • The corresponding result and confidence interval.
  • The result emphasized in the headline and whether it is the same result.
  • Any reported amendments and the explanation supplied.

See Cochrane Handbook Chapter 8, section 8.7. Use the separate outcome-measure guide to check what kind of learning claim an assessment addresses.
