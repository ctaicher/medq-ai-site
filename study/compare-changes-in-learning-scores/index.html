Separate score improvement from a study-method comparison

“Scores improved after using the app” describes a change over time. To judge whether a learning method helped more than the comparison method, read the results for both groups and identify the analysis used to compare them.

Keep three calculations separate

In this fictional study, residents are randomly assigned to Method A or Method B. Each group has the same residents complete a 100-point assessment before teaching and at four weeks. For this example, every enrolled resident completes both assessments.

Fictional mean scores on the same 100-point scale
GroupBeforeFour weeksMean change
Method A6080+20 points
Method B6478+14 points
  • Change within Method A is 80 − 60 = 20 points.
  • Difference in final scores is 80 − 78 = 2 points favoring Method A.
  • Difference in mean changes is (80 − 60) − (78 − 64) = 6 points favoring Method A.

These are different summaries of the same table. The 20-point gain alone does not describe an advantage over Method B. The groups also started four points apart, which is why the two between-group calculations differ in this sample.

Find the planned analysis

Check the methods and, when available, the protocol or analysis plan. Was the primary comparison based on final scores, changes, or a model adjusting follow-up scores for baseline? Report the actual estimate and its confidence interval from that analysis. This table has no variability data, so it cannot establish statistical significance or supply a confidence interval.

Cochrane’s handbook distinguishes post-intervention values from changes and explains that the participants with baseline and follow-up measurements may differ. If some residents miss the assessment, check who contributes to each mean before subtracting one reported mean from another.

Write a conclusion that preserves the comparison

For the example, write “Mean scores increased in both groups. The observed gain was 20 points with Method A and 14 with Method B, a six-point difference in gains. Interpretation requires the planned between-group analysis and its uncertainty.” Avoid choosing whichever summary makes the method appear most favorable.

Use the Cochrane Handbook, section 6.5.2.1 for the distinction between final values and change scores. Continue with the guide to precision and follow-up participation example.
