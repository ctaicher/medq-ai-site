Read question-bank item statistics before revising an item

A worked example of item difficulty and discrimination for question-bank editors.

MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD

Start with the denominator

Suppose 60 learners submit a first response to one version of an item and 42 select the keyed option. The proportion correct is 42 ÷ 60, or 70%. This statistic describes performance in that group. Repeat attempts and responses to an earlier version answer different questions, so track them separately.

Compare performance groups

Suppose 15 of the 20 highest-scoring learners answer correctly, compared with 9 of the 20 lowest-scoring learners. The upper-minus-lower difference is 75% minus 45%, or 30 percentage points. Define the groups using scores on the other items where feasible. That keeps the item being evaluated from contributing to its own group assignment.

Decide what to investigate

Little or negative separation is a reason to inspect the key, wording, scoring and sample before deciding on a revision. An easy item can also be useful when it checks essential knowledge. Start a review with the two rates, the number of responses, the version, and a short note about who took the assessment.

The example is fictional. See the NBME Item-Writing Guide for assessment design and measurement guidance, and the MedQ-AI distractor audit for reviewing specific answer choices.
