Check a paper's update status before reusing a study note

A saved PDF or reading note captures what you saw at one point in time. Before using it to support a new teaching point, return to the publisher's record and check for linked corrections, retractions, or other updates. Then read what the notice actually changes.

Follow the record, not just the saved copy

Crossref's Crossmark FAQ explains that the presence of a Crossmark button alone does not mean a particular copy is up to date. Readers need to open it and read the status information. When an update is available, the record can direct them to the publisher's notice.

If a paper has no Crossmark button, check the publisher's article page for notices and related versions. Absence of the button does not settle the status. A status check also does not establish that a study's methods or conclusions are sound; those need their own appraisal.

Trace the change to the teaching point

In a fictional example, an educator's saved note says a knowledge assessment occurred four weeks after a session. The publisher later posts a correction saying the assessment occurred four days afterward. The educator had used the timing to describe longer-term retention.

The correction changes the support for that particular teaching claim. A useful revision is: “The assessment was at four days; this result should not be described as a four-week retention finding.” The example is invented. It does not describe a real paper, a MedQ-AI trial, or an update found in a study cited elsewhere on this site.

Keep an update record with the note

Identity
Record the original paper's title and DOI or another stable identifier.
Update
Link the notice and record its type and date. Read whether it applies to the passage, table, or outcome you used.
Consequence
Explain which part of the teaching note changes and why. Keep unaffected conclusions separate from claims that need revision.
Check date
Record when you reviewed the publisher's record and whether a current copy or notice could be accessed.

If the notice cannot be retrieved, record that limitation rather than assuming the change is minor. For a retraction, read the notice and reconsider relying on the affected work. Do not present a retracted finding as established current evidence.

This is an original reading workflow, not an announcement of automated correction tracking in MedQ-AI. Pair it with tracing a claim to its source and checking a study's planned outcome.
