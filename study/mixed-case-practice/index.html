Mix practice questions around a decision

A way to build a mixed study set that helps learners distinguish similar cases.

MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD

Choose the decisions to compare

Pick two or three decisions that learners tend to confuse. For each, write down the fact that should steer the choice. A study set works best when every question gives the learner a chance to explain that distinction.

Build a six-question set

Use two examples of each of three decisions. Keep the topics unlabeled and avoid a predictable order. After each response, have the learner name the clue and explain why it favors the chosen option. Save a fresh example for a later check.

Use the resulting errors to choose a follow-up

If the learner cannot recall the relevant rule, give a short refresher and try one new example. If they recall the rule but keep choosing the wrong category, place two contrasting examples side by side and ask what single change would shift the decision.

Research by Kornell and Bjork studied category learning with paintings. The exercise above adapts the idea for question-bank practice. See the two-week retention log to track what transfers to a new question.
