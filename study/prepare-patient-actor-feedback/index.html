Prepare patient actors to give specific feedback

A patient actor can tell a learner how an encounter felt from the role they portrayed. The Association of Standardized Patient Educators’ published standards include training actors to provide feedback. Prepare that conversation alongside the case script so the actor knows what the session is trying to teach.

Agree on the learning objective

In a fictional visit, the learner explains a follow-up plan and invites the person to ask questions. Give the actor the scenario, the learning objective, and the format for feedback. Have a faculty member review which comments belong to the actor’s experience and which clinical questions need faculty input.

Ask the actor to notice one moment that affected their ability to participate. In a rehearsal, the learner might turn toward a computer immediately after asking, “Any questions?” The actor can describe what they noticed and how it affected their response.

Rehearse a concrete observation

A possible comment is “When you asked whether I had questions, you turned back to the screen before I answered. I felt that the conversation was ending, so I kept my question to myself.” Ask the learner what they understood from that feedback and what they would try in the next encounter.

Have the actor rehearse the comment with an educator before using it with learners. Check that the observation is specific, the actor speaks from the portrayed person’s perspective, and the language gives the learner room to respond. An actor who did not experience that concern should describe the response they actually had during the scene.

Give feedback on the next attempt

Repeat the end of the conversation with a fresh question from the actor. Ask whether the learner gave the person enough time and attention to raise it. Let the actor say which part helped and which part still felt difficult. Check in with the actor afterward about the portrayal and the feedback session.
