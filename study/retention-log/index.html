A two-week retention log for board prep

A practical way to track whether a missed concept holds up on a fresh question two weeks later.

Published by MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD

Record the distinction you missed

After a board-style question, write a short cue and the distinction it tested. A useful entry fits in one sentence. For example, “I read a change in timing as a change in severity.” Then write a prompt you can answer without reopening the original item. Ask yourself what timing detail would reverse your interpretation.

Use a different example for each check

Two days later, answer your prompt from memory and write a reason before opening the explanation. After roughly two weeks, look for a fresh vignette that turns on the same distinction. Mark the concept as recalled, recalled with a cue, or missed again. These intervals are a sample routine you can adjust to your study schedule.

Decide what to study again

Use two separate tallies for recall and application. A learner can remember an explanation and still miss the cue in a new question. When that happens, compare the two question stems and underline the detail that signals the same rule. The log gives you a concrete reason to revisit a concept in a later mixed set.
