Update a probability with a likelihood ratio

An interactive worked example for converting a starting probability and likelihood ratio into a revised probability.

A likelihood ratio measures how much more or less often a given result occurs in people with the condition than in people without it. To apply a likelihood ratio to a starting probability, convert the probability to odds first.

Revised probability: 50%

Work the default example by hand

  1. Convert 20% to 0.20 ÷ 0.80 = 0.25 in odds.
  2. Multiply 0.25 by the likelihood ratio of 4 to get odds of 1.
  3. Convert the new odds to 1 ÷ (1 + 1) = 50%.

Try a result that lowers probability

Set the likelihood ratio to 0.25 and keep the same starting probability. The new odds are 0.0625, giving a revised probability of about 5.9%. A likelihood ratio of 1 leaves the estimate where it started.

These inputs are fictional. For real results, use likelihood ratios estimated for the relevant test, threshold, and population.
