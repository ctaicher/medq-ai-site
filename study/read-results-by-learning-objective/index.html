Read practice results by learning objective

Two learners can have the same score and need to practice different skills. If you are planning a follow-up session, keep track of what each question asked the learner to do.

Start with one objective per question

In this fictional exercise, 10 questions ask learners to identify the most useful fact for comparing competing diagnoses. Ten ask them to interpret a diagnostic test result. Review the question and the expected reasoning before assigning it to an objective. If a question depends equally on several skills, flag it for a closer look.

Review the answers before assigning more practice

Ask Learner A to explain two missed test-interpretation answers. If both explanations use the wrong comparison group, try a fresh question where identifying that group is the first step. Ask Learner B which facts changed their ranking of diagnoses. If they could name the diagnoses but missed the distinguishing feature, practice choosing one discriminating question or exam finding in a new case.

These small sets are prompts for review. Ten items can cover different case types, and a missed item may reflect wording or unfamiliar context. Use the answer explanations, then see whether the same reasoning shows up on a different case before expanding the study assignment.

Use the objective when you write the next question

Keep the reasoning task stable and change the details of the case. In his EM Resident article on AI in medical education, Christopher Taicher, MD, discusses making learning more responsive to individual needs. The exercise here shows how an educator can check a proposed focus against a learner’s work.
