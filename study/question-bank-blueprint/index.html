Build a question-bank blueprint

A worked blueprint that maps emergency medicine learning objectives to decisions, cases, and feedback.

From MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD. Carnegie Mellon lists him on the MedQ AI team.

Start with what a learner should be able to do after the session. A topic label such as “handoffs” can describe a chapter. An objective such as “assign an owner for a pending result” tells you what the question must ask the learner to decide.

Plan the decisions before writing the stems

Here is a fictional eight-question set for emergency medicine interns. The counts illustrate a local teaching plan. They can change with the time available and the learners’ needs.

Check coverage at the item level

  1. Give every item one primary objective. Count it once even if its vignette includes context from another row.
  2. Write the decision and the fact that makes one choice best. Replace draft items whose answer depends on a workflow the stem never specifies.
  3. Reserve a fresh scenario for each objective. Change the people and setting while preserving the reasoning you want to see.

For a quick audit, have another educator assign each item to a row without seeing its label. If both of you classify a “time window” item as “assign responsibility,” revise the lead-in or move the item and fill the gap.
