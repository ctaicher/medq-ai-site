Read who was included in a learning-study analysis

Two analyses of the same learning study can answer different questions. Before comparing the headline percentages, identify who entered each calculation and whether learners stayed in the groups to which they were assigned.

Start with the assignment and outcome record

In a fictional study, 100 learners are randomly assigned to two teaching formats: 50 to Format A and 50 to Format B. Every learner completes the same follow-up assessment, so outcome data are available for all 100. Some learners do not attend all the assigned teaching. All numbers below are invented for this example, not MedQ-AI results.

The second row excludes ten learners from each original group. The equal number excluded does not establish that the remaining groups are comparable. Time available, prior preparation, or other factors could be associated with attendance and the assessment outcome.

Separate assignment from adherence

An intention-to-treat analysis estimates the effect of assignment by retaining randomized groups and including all randomized participants. A per-protocol effect concerns adherence to the specified intervention. Simply restricting a calculation to people who attended everything can introduce bias; it does not automatically estimate that effect reliably.

For the fictional study, the first row supports a statement about the observed difference after assignment. The second describes a selected subset. Its larger difference does not establish that attending all sessions caused a larger benefit. The table provides no confidence intervals and is insufficient for a claim about the precision of either difference.

Follow the people behind the label

  • How many learners were assigned to each format?
  • Were they analyzed in their original groups?
  • Who was excluded, and was exclusion based on something that happened after assignment?
  • Were outcomes measured for everyone, including learners who stopped attending?
  • Which effect did the investigators plan to estimate?

Missing assessments require a separate review. Calling an analysis “intention to treat” does not make absent outcomes reappear or resolve bias from missing data. Read the actual inclusion rules and methods used to address missing values.

The distinction follows Cochrane Handbook Chapter 8, section 8.2.2. This original numerical example is a reading aid, not a complete statistical appraisal. Continue with reading follow-up in a learning study and randomization, concealment, and blinding.
