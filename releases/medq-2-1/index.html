MedQ expands into Family Medicine and General Surgery

MedQ’s version 2.1.0 is available on Apple’s U.S. App Store under the title MedQ: Evolve with the Evidence. The release brings Family Medicine and General Surgery into the app alongside Emergency Medicine.

What is available

The public listing describes 165 multiple-choice questions in each new specialty: 15 free questions and three optional packs of 50, purchased individually. Emergency Medicine has its own catalog. Staged cases and questions with a changed vignette detail help learners examine how new information affects an answer. Study and timed modes support different review sessions; articles and videos connect with topics identified for further study.

A first session with a purpose

Choose one question you can explain afterward, rather than judging the entire session by a total score. Keep a short note outside the app with three entries:

  1. The deciding detail. Write which finding or piece of the timeline mattered most to your answer. Name the detail before rereading the explanation.
  2. The alternative you considered. Explain why another option initially seemed plausible and which information made it less suitable.
  3. The next question to investigate. Turn an uncertainty into a focused reading task. For example: “Which population and setting does the explanation’s supporting study describe?”

When a case changes, return to your first explanation. Mark the premise that changed and state whether your original reason still holds. This gives you something concrete to discuss with a study partner or educator, including when your first answer was correct.

Connect the resource to the question

Before opening several recommended resources, decide what you want the first one to clarify. After reading or watching, write a sentence answering that question and keep the original source link. If a summary refers to a research paper, inspect the paper’s population, outcome, and limitations before carrying the claim into a teaching note.
