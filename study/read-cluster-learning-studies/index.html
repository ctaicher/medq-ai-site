Read education studies that assign whole programs

An education study may assign a whole residency program to a new learning method. When you read the results, identify both what was randomized and whose outcomes were measured.

Draw the assignment before counting learners

In a fictional study, eight programs each enroll 20 residents. Four programs are randomly assigned to adaptive question practice and four to fixed-sequence practice. Both groups have the same planned study time and explanation format. All 160 residents are assessed after the learning period.

Ask what residents share within a program

Residents may learn from the same faculty, attend the same conferences, and discuss questions together. Their outcomes can therefore be related. Look for an analysis that accounts for residents being grouped within programs. Cochrane’s handbook explains that ignoring clustering can make confidence intervals too narrow and the results appear more precise than the design supports.

Check who entered the study and when

Find out whether residents were identified before or after their program’s assignment was known. Ask whether knowing the assigned method could have affected who was invited or who chose to participate. Check how many programs and residents completed follow-up and why any were missing.

For a journal-club discussion, write the unit of assignment beside the reported sample size. Then identify where the methods address within-program similarity. A participant count of 160 describes the residents observed. The eight randomized programs also matter when interpreting the result.

See Cochrane Handbook Chapter 23 for cluster-randomized study guidance. Pair this example with the comparison-group guide and follow-up participation guide.
