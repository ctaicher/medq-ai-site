Write a question learners can answer before seeing the options

The lead-in is the question learners see at the end of a vignette. A focused lead-in tells them what kind of answer they need to supply. In its item-writing tutorial, NBME calls this the cover-the-options rule. Ask a reviewer to hide the answer choices and try to answer from the vignette and lead-in alone.

Keep the vignette and rewrite the question

In a fictional research exercise, a test returns 30 positive results. A reference standard shows that 24 of those people have the condition. The exercise is intended to assess whether learners can calculate positive predictive value.

  • First draft. What can be concluded about this test?
  • Revision. Among the people who tested positive, what percentage had the condition according to the reference standard?

With the first draft, a learner may supply a statement about incorrect results, a fraction, or a general observation about test performance. With the revision, the learner can identify a numerator of 24 and a denominator of 30 and calculate 80% before seeing an option. If the learning objective is to distinguish predictive value from other metrics, give the learner the full two-by-two table and tighten the wording to ask for the named metric.

Make a second reviewer try it

  1. Give the reviewer only the stem and lead-in. Have them write down the expected type of answer and the fact that supports it.
  2. Compare their answer with the learning objective. If they choose a different denominator or interpret the task differently, ask what in the text led them there.
  3. Show the options. Keep the units and level of detail consistent across choices. If you intend to diagnose a misconception, record the specific reasoning each wrong answer would represent.

Use the question review record to carry the revision forward. After learners have used the item, a distractor audit can help determine which wrong answers warrant a closer look.
