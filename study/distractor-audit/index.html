Audit distractors using learner responses

A checklist for deciding when a frequently selected wrong option reveals a misconception or a problem with the question.

MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD

Read the item before the dashboard

Give a reviewer the stem and choices without the key or response counts. Ask them to solve the item, name the decisive clue, and explain why the strongest alternative loses. Record any assumption the reviewer had to make.

Investigate the most common wrong answer

A popular wrong answer may reflect a common misunderstanding, ambiguous wording, or an outdated explanation. Compare the response pattern with what reviewers heard from learners. Keep counts tied to the item version and note how many responses are represented. Treat a small sample as a prompt to investigate.

For example, if learners chose the same distractor because the stem left the timing unclear, revise the timing and verify the key again. If timing was explicit and learners misread its significance, consider adding a short explanation of why the alternative was tempting.

Compare the next version carefully

Keep the original and revised counts separate. Record the wording change, who approved it, and the question the change was intended to answer. Compare response patterns only after considering whether the learners and testing conditions are comparable.

See the NBME Item-Writing Guide for guidance on stems and options and the MedQ-AI question-review record for a way to document changes.
