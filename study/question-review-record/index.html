A review record for AI-drafted medical questions

A record template for clinician educators to document the evidence, revision, and disposition of a practice question.

Published by MedQ-AI, founded by Christopher Taicher, MD

Give each review a traceable record

Save an item ID, the version the reviewer saw, the learning objective, the selected answer, and the source used to verify it. Record the source date or edition and the relevant section so another reviewer can locate it. Keep an explicit decision such as approved, revise and recheck, or retire.

  • Question and version — item ID, draft version, and review date.
  • Clinical claim — sentence or explanation being checked and its supporting source.
  • Revision — original wording, approved wording, and reviewer rationale.
  • Disposition — review decision and any remaining issue.

Record an ambiguity in context

Suppose a reviewer finds that a question asks for the “next step” without saying whether the initial evaluation has started. Two options could be defensible, depending on that assumption. The review record can identify the missing fact, document the wording added to clarify the stage of care, and record a fresh review of all answer choices.

A second reader can then determine whether the revised question tests the original objective. A record with only “fixed” gives that reader too little information to reproduce the decision.

Use the record when the item changes

Keep the record tied to the version learners saw. Recheck edited explanations and new citations before releasing a revised item. If a learner flags a question, compare their comment with the exact version and update the record with the finding.

The NBME Item-Writing Guide provides structural guidance for stems and options. The NIST Generative AI Profile provides broader guidance on evaluating risks from generated material.
