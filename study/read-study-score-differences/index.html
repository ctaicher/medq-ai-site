Read a study score difference in percentage points

A difference between quiz scores can be reported in percentage points or as a relative percentage. Those expressions use different calculations. Reporting the original scores and the time of assessment lets readers interpret either one.

Start with the two published scores

In a randomized study of pediatric and emergency medicine residents, Larsen, Butler, and Roediger compared repeated testing with repeated study after a teaching session. On the final assessment more than six months after the original session, mean scores were 39% for material in the repeated-testing condition and 26% for material in the repeated-study condition.

Two ways to express the same difference using the rounded scores
MeasureCalculationResult
Absolute difference39% − 26%13 percentage points
Relative difference(39 − 26) ÷ 26About 50% higher relative to repeated study

“About 50% higher” can sound large without the starting score. Both expressions describe the same comparison. The 13-point difference is a difference in mean scores between learning conditions. It cannot tell you how many individual residents improved or what happened on shift.

Write the result so someone else can check it

Try “On the assessment more than six months after teaching, average scores were 39% with repeated testing and 26% with repeated study, a difference of 13 percentage points.” If you add a relative comparison, give the reference score. Before comparing findings across papers, check that they measured the same outcome at a similar point after teaching.
